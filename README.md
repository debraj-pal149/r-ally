# r-ally

**The app that shouts at you at the exact moment you want to quit — so you don't.**

r-ally is a workout companion. It watches your live effort signals (heart rate, pace, cadence, power, rep velocity, motion), detects the moment your body starts negotiating with your brain, and — right then, not 30 seconds later — a voice with the energy of Rocky Balboa, Les Brown, or a drill sergeant erupts in your ears with a short, context-aware, personally-relevant pep line generated by Claude and spoken aloud via text-to-speech.

This document is the **complete, end-to-end implementation specification**. An implementing agent should be able to build the entire system — the iOS/macOS app **and** the wearable activity simulator — from this document alone, without asking questions. Read all of it before writing any code. Everything is specified: product intent, UX, algorithms with formulas and starting constants, network protocols with JSON schemas, file trees, exact libraries, prompts, config/secrets handling, build commands, test plans, acceptance criteria, and known gotchas.

## 1. Product vision & the one core insight

### The problem

Every workout has a moment — usually more than one — where the athlete's output starts to sag *before* they consciously decide to stop. The legs slow a fraction. The cadence stutters. Heart rate keeps climbing while pace drops. The bar moves slower on rep 7 than rep 3. That window between "body starts fading" and "brain gives itself permission to quit" is typically **15–60 seconds** long. A well-timed, emotionally-charged external voice inside that window is the single highest-leverage intervention in amateur fitness. Coaches do it. Corner men do it. Crowds at mile 25 do it. Nobody's phone does it.

### The insight that makes this buildable

**You cannot directly measure "wants to quit." You can measure effort decay relative to the athlete's own recent baseline** — and the physiological signature of struggling (output falling *while* heart rate stays high or rises) is distinguishable from the signature of a deliberate stop (output falls *and* HR is allowed to fall, e.g. cooldown or a traffic light). r-ally's core is a **Quit-Risk Engine**: a small, interpretable, per-activity signal-fusion model that outputs a 0–100 risk score in real time and fires an encouragement trigger with rate-limiting and hysteresis. It is not ML in v1 — it is well-tuned classical signal processing, which is testable, explainable, and tunable via the simulator's ground-truth labels.

### The product feeling (as designed by a PM/designer/athlete)

- **Audio-first.** During a workout, the phone is in a pocket or armband. The screen is secondary. The product IS the voice. The screen exists for setup, for glancing, and for the post-workout story.
- **It should feel like a corner man, not a notification.** No chimes, no "r-ally says:". Music ducks, the voice punches through, music comes back. Two seconds of theater.
- **The moment must be right.** One perfectly-timed line beats ten scheduled ones. If r-ally speaks when the athlete feels fine, trust dies instantly. This is why the Quit-Risk Engine is the core IP, and why the whole simulator exists — to tune the timing until it's uncanny.
- **The post-workout screen tells a story of character, not just stats:** "Kilometer 3.4 — you were fading. You rallied. That's who you are now." These are called **Rally Moments** and they are the emotional retention loop of the app.
- **The name:** r-ally = "rally" (to rally, a rallying cry) + "ally" (it's on your side). UI copy uses "Rally" as the verb everywhere: the trigger event is a *rally*, the summary shows *rally moments*, the big button says **"Let's Rally."**

### Non-goals for v1

- No accounts, no backend, no social features, no streaming music integration.
- No on-device ML model training. Classical signal processing only, tuned against simulator ground truth.
- No Android.

## 2. What we are building (two artifacts)

### Artifact A — the r-ally app

A **SwiftUI iOS app** (single iOS target, iOS 17.0 minimum deployment, built with Xcode 26.5 / iOS 26 SDK) that:

- Runs on a real iPhone for real outdoor/indoor runs using the phone's own sensors (GPS pace, pedometer cadence, motion classification) — **no watch required for running**.
- Runs on the Mac for demos via the **iOS Simulator** (primary demo path) and also as "Designed for iPhone" on Apple Silicon. In these modes it consumes data from Artifact B over a local WebSocket.
- Abstracts all inputs behind a `WorkoutDataSource` protocol so the app is *identical* whether data comes from real sensors, HealthKit, a Bluetooth heart-rate strap, or the simulator. This is how we support "any exercise whatsoever" without owning a watch: **the simulator is a first-class device.**
- Runs the Quit-Risk Engine at 1 Hz, calls the Anthropic API (`claude-haiku-4-5`) to generate persona-styled pep lines, and speaks them with `AVSpeechSynthesizer` while ducking other audio.
- Reports its internal risk score and every trigger back to the simulator over the same WebSocket so the simulator dashboard can visualize *both* the fake vitals *and* the app's reactions on one timeline.
- Saves completed workouts to HealthKit and can upload to Strava (post-workout — that is all Strava's API allows; see §6).

### Artifact B — the activity simulator ("virtual Garmin")

A **TypeScript Node server + React dashboard** (in `simulator/`) that:

- Generates physiologically-plausible live streams (HR, pace, cadence, power, stroke rate, punch rate, rep velocity, motion intensity…) for **7 activity types**: running, cycling, strength training, boxing, swimming, rowing, HIIT.
- Runs **scripted scenarios** (JSON DSL) containing labeled **ground-truth quit events** — the athlete fades at minute 14, hard-stops at km 3.2, grinds through rep 8 — which are *hidden from the app* but shown on the dashboard and used to score the app's detection accuracy.
- Streams samples to the app over WebSocket at 1 Hz (configurable), with a **time-scale control (1×–30×)** so a 40-minute run can be demoed in 90 seconds.
- Renders a **live dashboard**: multi-series charts of every vital, the app's live risk score overlaid, vertical markers for ground-truth events vs. app triggers, a transcript of every spoken line, and manual "puppeteer" sliders to inject fatigue live during a demo.
- Has a **"rescuable athlete" mode**: if the app's encouragement arrives within the rescue window of a fade event, the simulated athlete *recovers* — effort climbs back. This makes demos emotionally legible: you literally watch the pep talk save the run.
- Can **export deterministic CSV fixtures** (seeded RNG) used by the Swift unit tests, and **score eval runs** (lead time, recall, false positives) into a report.

```
On a real iPhone run: DeviceSensorDataSource (+BLE strap if present) replaces

```

**Key architectural rules:**

1. **Everything is a `MetricSample`.** Every input — GPS, pedometer, simulator, BLE, HealthKit — is normalized to `MetricSample(kind, value, timestamp, source)` before anything else sees it. Canonical kinds & units are defined once (§9, mirrored in Swift and TypeScript).
2. **The engine never knows where data came from.** No `if simulator` branches downstream of `MetricStore`. This is the entire testing strategy.
3. **The app is the only Anthropic client.** The simulator never touches the LLM.
4. **The feedback channel is part of the product.** The app always publishes `risk` and `trigger` events to its data source if that source is the simulator; this powers the dashboard overlay and the eval scorer.
5. **1 Hz is the engine tick.** All metrics are resampled to a 1-second grid inside `MetricStore`. Sensors may deliver faster/slower; the engine always sees a clean 1 Hz frame.

## 5. The Quit-Risk Engine (the core IP)

### 5.1 Theory of operation

We detect three distinct situations, each with its own trigger type:

| Trigger kind | Signature | What the voice should do |
|---|---|---|
| `pre_quit_fade` | Output (pace/power/velocity/punch-rate) decays vs. the athlete's own rolling baseline **while HR stays high or rises** (cardiac decoupling). The classic "negotiating with yourself" pattern. | Interrupt the negotiation. Highest-energy line. This is the money moment. |
| `stopped` | Output collapses to ~zero within seconds while HR is still elevated, and the stop persists past a grace period (i.e., not a traffic light). | "Get back in it" — firm, warm, immediate. |
| `grind_support` | No decay, but sustained very hard effort (long time above threshold). The athlete is holding on and deserves fuel, not correction. | Pure recognition + fuel. Positive reinforcement. |
| `final_push` | ≥ 90% of a stated goal (distance/time/rounds) reached. | Bring it home. |

And two situations where we must **stay silent**:

- **Deliberate cooldown / planned rest:** output falls *and HR is falling too*, or the fall happens inside a known rest interval (between sets/rounds), or after the goal is complete.
- **Micro-pauses:** traffic lights, water sips, chalk-up. Output collapses but resumes within the grace window.

### 5.2 Signals per activity (what the adapter feeds the core)

Every activity adapter reduces its raw metrics to the same three normalized channels consumed by the shared core:

- **`output`** ∈ [0,1] — the primary work-rate signal, normalized to the session's rolling baseline.
- **`hr`** — heart rate in bpm (may be absent; core degrades gracefully).
- **`rhythm`** — a periodicity/steadiness signal (cadence, stroke rate, punch rate, rep tempo) whose *variability* is informative.

| Activity | `output` from | `rhythm` from | Structure awareness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Running | speed (1/pace) | cadence (spm) | none (continuous) |
| Cycling | power (watts), fallback speed | pedal cadence | none |
| Strength | rep velocity (m/s per rep) | rep tempo / inter-rep gap | **set/rest segmentation** — decay is judged *within a set*; rest between sets is planned silence (but rest overruns > 1.5× the athlete's median rest can fire a gentle `stopped`) |
| Boxing | punch rate (punches/min) | punch rate regularity | **round/rest segmentation** (3:00/1:00 default) |
| Swimming | speed from lap splits, stroke rate | stroke rate | lap segmentation; expect HR dropouts (optical HR fails in water — simulator models this) |
| Rowing | power / stroke pace | stroke rate (spm) | none |
| HIIT / generic | motion intensity (accelerometer RMS, g) | dominant motion frequency | work/rest interval segmentation if the user declared a timer |

This table is the answer to *"how do you detect stopping across every sport with high accuracy"*: you don't build seven detectors — you build **one detector over (output, hr, rhythm)** plus thin per-sport adapters that (a) pick the right raw metrics and (b) know the sport's planned rest structure so planned pauses never fire.

### 5.3 The core algorithm (implement exactly this, constants are starting points)

`MetricStore` maintains, per channel, on the 1 Hz grid:

- `base(t)` — EWMA with **90 s half-life** (α = 1 − 2^(−1/90) ≈ 0.00767 per tick): the athlete's "recent normal".
- `short(t)` — 15 s simple moving average: "right now".
- `slope30(t)` — least-squares slope over the trailing 30 s, in units/s.
- `cv20(t)` — coefficient of variation of `rhythm` over trailing 20 s.

Features each tick (all clamped to [0,1] with the scales given):

```
outputDrop  = clamp( (base_out − short_out) / max(base_out, ε) , 0, 1 )          // 0.15 means 15% below normal
decaySlope  = clamp( −slope30_out / (0.005 · base_out) , 0, 1 )                  // full score at 0.5%/s decay
decoupling  = (hr present) ? clamp( z(hrTrend) , 0, 1 ) · step(outputDrop > 0.05) : 0
              where hrTrend = slope30_hr in bpm/s; z scales 0.05 bpm/s → 1.0
cooldownCue = (hr present) ? clamp( −slope30_hr / 0.15 , 0, 1 ) : (goalDone ? 1 : 0)
stutter     = clamp( (cv20_rhythm − base_cv) / 0.10 , 0, 1 )
grind       = clamp( secondsAboveHardEffort / 300 , 0, 1 )                       // hard = HR > 88% max, or output > 0.92·base sustained
```

Risk (logistic fusion, then smoothed):

```
raw   = 100 · σ( 3.2·outputDrop + 2.0·decaySlope + 2.4·decoupling + 1.2·stutter
                 + 0.6·grind − 2.8·cooldownCue − 2.2 )         // σ = logistic
risk  = 0.7·risk_prev + 0.3·raw                                 // EWMA smoothing
```

State machine with hysteresis (evaluated on `risk`):

```
CRUISING  ── risk ≥ 45 for 3 consecutive ticks ──▶ WOBBLING
WOBBLING  ── risk ≥ 65 for 3 consecutive ticks ──▶ CRITICAL   → fire pre_quit_fade
WOBBLING  ── risk ≤ 35 for 10 ticks ──▶ CRUISING
CRITICAL  ── risk ≤ 50 for 15 ticks ──▶ WOBBLING
```

**Sudden-stop path (parallel to the state machine):** if `short_out < 0.15·base_out` and the collapse happened in < 4 s → enter `PAUSED_UNKNOWN` and start an **8-second grace timer**. If output resumes ≥ 0.5·base within grace → silent, return to previous state (traffic light). If grace expires, HR (if present) is > 75% of session peak, and the goal isn't done → fire `stopped`. In strength/boxing, the grace window is instead "planned rest median × 1.5".

**Suppression rules (checked before any trigger fires):**

1. Warmup mask: no triggers in the first **180 s** of the session.
2. Refractory period: ≥ **90 s** since the last spoken line (any kind).
3. Session cap: max **7** triggers per session; `grind_support` max 2; `final_push` max 1.
4. Post-goal: after the declared goal completes, only `final_push`/celebration, never `pre_quit_fade`.
5. Resume grace: 30 s of silence after any `stopped` recovery (let them settle back in).

**Escalation:** if a second `pre_quit_fade` fires within 5 minutes of the first, set `intensity = "maximum"` in the LLM context (§7.4) — the voice goes up a register.

### 5.4 Why this beats alternatives (decisions already debated — do not relitigate)

- **Why not an ML classifier?** No labeled real-world data exists yet; a tuned interpretable model + simulator ground truth gives measurable precision *now* and produces the labeled dataset (real workouts + "did the user actually stop") that a future model needs. The engine's features ARE the future model's features.
- **Why relative-to-own-baseline instead of absolute zones?** Absolute thresholds require knowing the athlete's fitness. Baselines self-calibrate within ~3 minutes of any session for any human at any fitness level. (HR max for zone math: default `211 − 0.64·age`, user-overridable.)
- **Why 1 Hz?** Quit windows are 15–60 s; 1 Hz is 15–60 samples of warning — plenty. Higher rates burn battery and add noise, not signal.
- **Why hysteresis + refractory instead of firing on every threshold cross?** A false or repeated shout is trust-fatal (§1). The eval (§17) scores false positives as heavily as misses.

**`DataSourceManager` fusion rules:** the user selects a *primary* source per workout (Settings default: Simulator in DEBUG, iPhone sensors in RELEASE). ## 7. The Encouragement Engine (LLM + voice)

### 7.1 Pipeline & latency budget

```
risk crosses 35 (WOBBLING) ──▶ PREFETCH: one Anthropic call returns 3 candidate
                               lines per plausible trigger kind, cached (TTL 240 s)
trigger fires ──▶ pop best cached line ──▶ AVSpeechSynthesizer speaks (< 700 ms
                               from trigger to first syllable) ──▶ fire-and-forget
                               refill call ──▶ report `trigger` + `speechDone` to simulator
no cache / offline / API error ──▶ FallbackLines bank (≥ 40 lines × persona,
                               context-slotted templates) — the shout NEVER fails
```

- Model: **`claude-haiku-4-5`** (alias; sub-second TTFT, $1/$5 per MTok). Do not use a bigger model — latency is the product.
- One prefetch call generates a JSON array of candidates for `pre_quit_fade`, `stopped`, and `grind_support` in a single response (cheap, amortized).
- Cache invalidation: TTL 240 s, or persona switch, or context drift (> 500 m distance / > 3 min elapsed since generation / segment change in structured sports).
- `max_tokens: 300`, `temperature: 1.0`.

### 7.2 Anthropic API call (exact shape)

`POST https://api.anthropic.com/v1/messages` with headers `x-api-key: <ANTHROPIC_API_KEY>`, `anthropic-version: 2023-06-01`, `content-type: application/json`. Plain `URLSession` — **no SDK dependency**.

```json
{
  "model": "claude-haiku-4-5",
  "max_tokens": 300,
  "temperature": 1.0,
  "system": "<persona system prompt, §7.4>",
  "messages": [{ "role": "user", "content": "<context block, §7.5>" }]
}
```

Response text must be a JSON object (prompt enforces it); parse defensively — on any parse failure, use fallback bank. Timeout 5 s. Never retry synchronously in the trigger path.

### 7.3 Personas (the voices)

Five personas. Legally & tastefully these are *inspired by* archetypes, never claiming to be the real people. Each has: display name, tagline, emoji-free style rules, `AVSpeech` voice settings, and a fallback line bank.

| id | Name | Archetype | Style rules (enforced in system prompt) | Speech settings |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `southpaw` | **The Southpaw** | Rocky-style corner man | Gravel and heart. Short punches of words. "One more round" energy. Calls you "kid" or "champ". Imperfect grammar welcome. | rate 0.50, pitch 0.85 |
| `machine` | **The Machine** | Arnold-style icon | Playful dominance, absolute certainty, loves the pain, light humor. | rate 0.48, pitch 0.80 |
| `preacher` | **The Preacher** | Les Brown-style orator | Sermon cadence, "YOU decided…", rises to a peak, greatness/destiny vocabulary. | rate 0.53, pitch 1.0 |
| `sarge` | **Sarge** | Drill instructor | Barked, clipped, zero sympathy, secretly proud. Clean language always (no profanity, ever). | rate 0.60, pitch 0.95 |
| `steady` | **The Steady** | Zen coach | Calm, grounded, breath-anchored, mantra-like. For users who hate being yelled at. | rate 0.45, pitch 1.05 |

### 7.4 System prompt (use verbatim, with `{slots}` filled)

```
You are {persona_name}, {persona_archetype_description}. You are the in-ear
corner-man for an athlete mid-workout. Your ONLY job: produce ultra-short
spoken pep lines that make a fading human keep going RIGHT NOW.

STYLE RULES FOR THIS PERSONA:
{persona_style_rules}

HARD RULES (never break):
- Each line ≤ 12 words. It will be spoken aloud by text-to-speech.
- No emojis, no hashtags, no quotation marks, no stage directions, no numbers
  with decimals. Spell out small numbers.
- Never mention sensors, data, heart rate readings, apps, or AI.
- Weave in ONE concrete detail from the context when natural (distance left,
  round number, rep count, their stated reason) — never more than one.
- Never repeat or closely paraphrase any line listed in recent_lines.
- No profanity, no body-shaming, no health risks ("push through the chest pain"
  is FORBIDDEN — if context says intensity maximum, get louder, not dangerous).
- Match trigger kind: pre_quit_fade = interrupt the doubt; stopped = get them
  moving again, firm but warm; grind_support = pure fuel and recognition;
  final_push = bring it home.

OUTPUT FORMAT: a single JSON object, nothing else:
{"pre_quit_fade": ["line1","line2","line3"],
 "stopped": ["line1","line2","line3"],
 "grind_support": ["line1","line2","line3"]}
```

### 7.5 Context block (the user message, filled each prefetch)

```
ATHLETE CONTEXT
- activity: running          - elapsed: 21m 40s
- progress: 3.4 km of 5.0 km goal (68%)
- session snapshot: pace slipping ~8% below their normal, cadence choppy,
  effort high for 4 straight minutes            ← plain-English, adapter-generated
- athlete's chosen encouragement prompt: "Remind me I'm training for my first 10K"
- athlete name to use (optional): "Deb"
- intensity: normal | maximum                   ← escalation flag, §5.3
- recent_lines: ["…", "…"]                      ← last 5 spoken lines
Generate the JSON now.
```

The "session snapshot" line is produced by the activity adapter as human phrasing (never raw numbers) — this is what makes lines feel psychic without ever mentioning data.

### 7.6 Encouragement prompt chips (pre-workout, per activity)

The user optionally picks ONE chip (or types a custom line, or picks none). Chips per activity — running shown in full; other sports get 5 analogous chips each (implementing agent: write them in the same spirit):

- "Remind me why I started"
- "I'm training for my first 10K"
- "Talk to me like it's the last round"
- "Tell me my legs are strong"
- "I run to be a good example for my kids"

### 7.7 Speech engine

## 8. The Activity Simulator

### 8.1 Physiology engine (equations — implement exactly, all constants in one `physiology.ts`)

The engine ticks at simulated 1 Hz (wall-clock scaled by `timeScale`). State per athlete profile `{age, hrRest, hrMax, thresholdPaceSecPerKm, ftpWatts, weightKg}` (defaults: 32 y, 58 bpm, 190 bpm, 330 s/km, 220 W, 72 kg).

**Effort → the scenario provides a target effort e(t) ∈ [0,1]** (piecewise segments + events, §8.2). Everything derives from effort:

```
Heart rate (first-order lag + drift + noise):
  hrTarget = hrRest + e_eff · (hrMax − hrRest) + drift
  drift    = 0.5 bpm/min · minutesElapsed · max(0, e_eff − 0.6) / 0.4        // cardiac drift
  τ        = rising ? 35 s : 60 s
  hr      += (hrTarget − hr) · (1 − exp(−dt/τ)) + 𝒩(0, 1.2)
  artifacts: with p=0.004/s, a ±8–15 bpm spike lasting 2–3 s (optical HR noise)

Fatigue (W′-balance-lite):
  criticalEffort = 0.75
  fatigue += (e(t) − criticalEffort) · dt / 900        when e > critical      // ~15 min to full at e=0.95... scaled
  fatigue −= (criticalEffort − e(t)) · dt / 1800       when e < critical (recovery)
  fatigue  = clamp(fatigue, 0, 1)
  e_eff    = e(t) · (1 − 0.25·fatigue)                 // fatigue erodes deliverable effort

Running:
  speed    = (1000/thresholdPace) · (0.55 + 0.60·e_eff) · gpsNoise(σ=2.5%)
  pace     = 1000 / speed
  cadence  = (150 + 32·e_eff) · (1 − 0.06·fatigue) + 𝒩(0, 1.5)
  pre-quit stutter: during a fade event, cadence σ triples (this is the tell)
  distance += speed·dt

Cycling:  power = ftp·(0.5 + 0.65·e_eff) + 𝒩(0, 8);  cadence = 78 + 18·e_eff;  speed = 8.1·(power/ftp)^(1/3) m/s

Strength (discrete): scenario declares sets [{reps, restSec, load}]. Per rep n of a set:
  repVelocity_n = v0 · (1 − vloss·(n−1)/(reps−1)) + 𝒩(0, 0.02),  v0=0.55 m/s,
  vloss ∈ [0.25, 0.45] rising with fatigue; near-failure reps get inter-rep gaps
  stretching 1.5 s → 4 s. HR sawtooths: e=0.85 during set, e=0.35 during rest.
  motionIntensity bursts per rep.

Boxing: rounds 180 s work / 60 s rest. punchRate = 55·(0.6+0.5·e_eff) ppm with
  intra-round decay; HR structure like strength but higher floor (e_rest=0.5).

Swimming: speed from lap splits (50 m laps), strokeRate = 28+16·e_eff spm.
  HR DROPOUTS: each sample dropped with p=0.15, plus 20–40 s full gaps (p=0.005/s
  to start one) — optical HR under water. The app must tolerate missing HR (§5.3).

Rowing: power = 0.8·ftp·(0.5+0.65·e_eff); strokeRate = 18+10·e_eff spm.

HIIT: work/rest intervals from scenario; motionIntensity = 0.15+0.85·e_eff g-RMS.
```

**Determinism:** all randomness through one seeded PRNG (`mulberry32`). Same seed + scenario ⇒ identical stream. This powers CSV fixtures and reproducible evals.

### 8.2 Scenario DSL (JSON, in `simulator/scenarios/*.json`)

```jsonc
{
  "id": "run-5k-bonk",
  "name": "5K race — bonk at 3.2 km",
  "activity": "running",
  "athlete": { "hrMax": 190, "thresholdPaceSecPerKm": 330 },
  "durationSec": 1800,
  "segments": [                                   // baseline effort profile
    { "untilSec": 180,  "effort": 0.55, "label": "warmup" },
    { "untilSec": 1500, "effort": 0.82, "label": "race pace" },
    { "untilSec": 1800, "effort": 0.60, "label": "cooldown" }   // must NOT trigger
  ],
  "events": [                                     // ground-truth quit events
    { "atSec": 690,  "kind": "gradual_fade", "rampSec": 75, "effortFloor": 0.45,
      "rescuable": true,  "rescueWindowSec": 45, "recoverySec": 30 },
    { "atSec": 1020, "kind": "sudden_stop",  "holdSec": 25, "rescuable": true,
      "rescueWindowSec": 20 },
    { "atSec": 480,  "kind": "pause_resume", "holdSec": 6 }     // traffic light — DISTRACTOR, app must stay silent
  ]
}
```

Event kinds the engine must implement: `gradual_fade` (effort ramps down over `rampSec` toward `effortFloor`), `sudden_stop` (effort→0 in 2 s, hold `holdSec`), `pause_resume` (short stop, auto-resume — distractor), `grind` (effort pinned at 0.93 for N sec — should elicit `grind_support`, not fade), `interval_rest` (planned — distractor, structured sports only). **Rescuable events:** if the app's `trigger` message arrives within `rescueWindowSec` of event onset, effort recovers to the segment baseline over `recoverySec` — the demo's magic moment. Non-rescued `sudden_stop`+`gradual_fade` at scenario end = the athlete quit.

**Ship ≥ 12 scenarios:** running ×4 (`run-easy-clean` no events, `run-5k-bonk`, `run-intervals` with planned rests as distractors, `run-gradual-fade-late`), cycling ×2 (`ride-tempo-fade`, `ride-surges`), strength ×2 (`lift-5x5-grind` with velocity-loss failure on set 4, `lift-fast-quit`), boxing ×1 (`box-6-rounds-fade-r5`), swimming ×1 (`swim-1500-dropouts`), rowing ×1, HIIT ×1.

### 8.3 Dashboard (React, `localhost:5173`)

Layout (dark theme matching the app, §11):

- **Header:** scenario picker · activity badge · time-scale slider (1×–30×) · Start/Pause/Reset · seed field · "rescuable athlete" toggle · connection badges ("app connected ●", "browser ●").
- **Main chart (uPlot, 60 fps):** stacked synced panes — HR (bpm, red), output pane (pace inverted so up=faster / power / velocity per activity, ember), cadence/rhythm (gray), **app risk score 0–100** (amber area, from `risk` messages). Vertical markers: dashed red = ground-truth event onsets (labeled), solid ember = app `trigger` (hover → the spoken text), gray band = speech duration (`trigger`→`speechDone`).
- **Puppeteer panel:** a live effort-override slider + "inject fade" / "hard stop" / "resume" buttons for manual demo control (these create ad-hoc ground-truth events too, so scoring still works).
- **Transcript panel:** every spoken line with timestamp, trigger kind, persona, LLM-vs-fallback badge, latency ms.
- **Eval panel (post-run):** table per ground-truth event — detected? lead time (s)? rescued? — plus totals: recall, mean lead time, false positives/10 min, verdict badge (§17). "Export report" writes `eval-reports/<scenario>-<ts>.json`.

## 9. The WebSocket protocol (app ⇄ simulator)

Single source of truth: `simulator/src/protocol.ts` (TypeScript types + JSON-schema comments), hand-mirrored in `app/.../Simulator/Protocol.swift` (Codable). Both files carry `PROTOCOL_VERSION = 1`.

**Endpoints:** `ws://localhost:7777/stream` (the app) and `ws://localhost:7777/dashboard` (the browser; receives everything on /stream **plus** ground truth & engine internals — ground truth must never flow to the app, that would be cheating).

**Canonical metric kinds & units** (string enum, both languages):
`heartRateBpm, paceSecPerKm, speedMps, cadenceSpm, powerWatts, strokeRateSpm, punchRatePpm, repVelocityMps, repCount, motionIntensityG, distanceM, activeEnergyKcal`.

Messages (`t` = simulated seconds since scenario start, float):

```jsonc
// simulator → app
{ "v":1, "type":"hello", "scenarioId":"run-5k-bonk", "activity":"running",
  "timeScale":10, "tickHz":1 }
{ "v":1, "type":"metrics", "t":412.0,
  "samples":[ {"k":"heartRateBpm","x":163.2}, {"k":"paceSecPerKm","x":341.0},
              {"k":"cadenceSpm","x":171.4}, {"k":"distanceM","x":1180.5} ] }
{ "v":1, "type":"scenarioEnded", "t":1800.0 }

// app → simulator
{ "v":1, "type":"appHello", "appVersion":"1.0", "persona":"southpaw" }
{ "v":1, "type":"risk", "t":688.0, "score":58.2, "state":"WOBBLING" }        // every 5 s, and on every state change
{ "v":1, "type":"trigger", "t":703.0, "kind":"pre_quit_fade",
  "persona":"southpaw", "text":"Kid, three K down. This hill ain't got nothing.",
  "sourceLLM":true, "latencyMs":214 }
{ "v":1, "type":"speechDone", "t":706.5 }
```

Transport rules: app uses `URLSessionWebSocketTask`; ping every 15 s; on disconnect retry with backoff 1→2→4→8 s (cap 8); app pauses the workout clock while disconnected. Unknown message types are ignored (forward compatibility). Server relays all `/stream` traffic to `/dashboard` verbatim, augmented with `{type:"groundTruth", ...}` and `{type:"engineDebug", effort, fatigue}` frames.

- **Primary CTA:** full-width 64-pt button — **"Let's Rally"** — ember gradient, springs on press.
- Below fold: last workout's Rally Moments teaser card → Summary.

### 10.2 Live Workout (the sacred screen)

- Pure black background (OLED). Center: **elapsed time**, SF Rounded, 76 pt, monospaced digits.
- Around it a **pulse ring** (240 pt Ø, 10 pt stroke) that beats at current HR (subtle 1.00→1.03 scale). Ring color = engine state: `CRUISING` calm green → `WOBBLING` amber → `CRITICAL` ember; transitions animate over 2 s. If no HR channel, ring pulses at cadence/2 instead.
- Under the ring: 3 metric tiles (activity-appropriate: pace·distance·cadence / power·distance·cadence / set·rep·velocity / round·punch-rate·clock …), 28 pt values, 11 pt uppercase labels.
- When a rally fires: screen dims 15 %, the spoken line types on in 34 pt italic serif ("New York" font) for the speech duration + 3 s, ring flares. This is the only time text overlays the screen.
- Bottom bar: pause/resume · end (long-press 1 s to end, with ring-fill affordance) · mute toggle (mute kills speech but the engine & logging run on).
- Screen stays awake (`isIdleTimerDisabled = true`).

### 10.3 Summary — "The story of the fight"

- Hero: activity, total time/distance, avg HR (if present).
- **Rally Moments timeline:** horizontal session strip (mini output sparkline); ember dots where rallies fired. Tapping a dot opens a card: *"14:32 — you were fading."* + the spoken line + what happened next ("Pace recovered 11 % within 40 s" — computed from `MetricStore` post-trigger deltas). If the user finished after a `CRITICAL`: badge **"You rallied N times and finished."**
- Buttons: Save to Apple Health (auto if authorized) · Upload to Strava (if connected) · Done.

### 10.4 History

Simple list (SwiftData-backed): date, activity, distance/duration, rally count. Tap → Summary (read-only).

### 10.5 Settings

Data source picker (Simulator / iPhone sensors / + BLE strap scan-and-pair sheet listing 0x180D devices) · simulator URL field (default `ws://localhost:7777/stream`) · voice picker + test · athlete profile (name, age, HR max override, threshold pace) · HealthKit authorize · Strava connect/disconnect · "Rally intensity" (max shouts per session 3–10, default 7) · About.

**First-run flow:** name → age → "How do you want to be pushed?" (persona with voice previews) → permissions primer sheets (motion/location/HealthKit each with one honest sentence of why) → Home.

## 17. Evaluation: how we measure that detection actually works

Scoring (`simulator/src/eval/scorer.ts`) matches app `trigger` messages against ground-truth events:

- A `gradual_fade`/`grind`/`sudden_stop` event is **detected** if a trigger of the matching kind fires in the window **[onset − 10 s, onset + 45 s]** (fade/grind) or **[onset + graceSec, onset + 30 s]** (stop). Lead time = onset − triggerTime (positive = we beat the quit).
- Any trigger outside all windows = **false positive**. Triggers during distractors (`pause_resume`, `interval_rest`, cooldown segments) = false positives **weighted ×2** (trust-killers).
- Rescue rate = rescued / rescuable-detected.

**v1 acceptance targets across the 12-scenario suite (seed 42, 10×):**

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Recall on fade/stop/grind events | ≥ 0.85 |
| Weighted false positives | ≤ 1 per 30 simulated minutes |
| Median detection latency after fade onset | ≤ 25 s (i.e., mid-fade, before the stop) |
| Distractor triggers (pauses, planned rests, cooldowns) | **0** across the suite |
| Trigger → first syllable | < 700 ms (LLM-cached) / < 150 ms (fallback) |

Tune only `EngineConstants.swift` to hit these; if a constant changes, re-run the whole suite (one dashboard button: "Run full eval" cycles all scenarios headlessly at 30× with the app connected).

## 19. Edge cases & gotchas (read before coding)

1. **ATS:** `ws://localhost` from the app requires `NSAllowsLocalNetworking` — without it the socket fails silently in RELEASE.
2. **iOS Simulator has no CoreMotion** and fake CoreLocation — never route Mac demos through `DeviceSensorDataSource`; DEBUG default source = Simulator.
3. **HealthKit is unavailable on native macOS** and its entitlement breaks "My Mac (Designed for iPhone)" runs — guard all HealthKit code `#if canImport(HealthKit)` and feature-flag at runtime; the iOS-Simulator demo path is unaffected (HealthKit works there).
4. **`AVAudioSession` activation can throw** when another app holds the session — catch, log, speak anyway (speech works without ducking).
5. **xcconfig comment trap:** any `//` inside the API key value would be eaten as a comment — the gen script must escape as `/$()/`.
6. **BLE HR flags byte:** bit 0 selects uint8 vs uint16 HR value — parse it, don't assume uint8.
7. **Optical-HR dropouts (swim scenario) are a feature:** the engine must produce sane risk with `hr` absent for 40 s (decoupling term → 0, others carry).
8. **Time-scale ≠ tick rate:** at 30× the server still emits 1 Hz *simulated* frames (30 msgs/wall-second); the app's `MetricStore` must be driven by **message `t`**, never wall clock, or every window/half-life breaks. This is the single most likely one-shot bug — the engine clock is simulated time throughout.
9. **`URLSessionWebSocketTask` receive loop** must re-arm after every message and handle `.data` and `.string` frames.
10. **Speech re-entrancy:** if a trigger fires while speaking (shouldn't, refractory ≥ 90 s, but belt-and-braces) — drop the new line, never queue shouts.
11. **CMPedometer cadence** arrives every ~2–5 s and can be nil early; hold last value, mark stale after 10 s.
12. **GPS pace spikes** at start / under bridges: clamp speed to [0.3, 12] m/s and require `horizontalAccuracy < 30 m` before accepting a location.
13. **Strava tokens expire in 6 h** — refresh on 401, and uploads are async server-side (poll the upload status endpoint before declaring success).
14. **Swift 6 strict concurrency:** sources are actors or `@MainActor`-isolated observables; `AsyncStream` continuations must be finished on `stop()` or streams leak.
15. **Idle timer & background:** `isIdleTimerDisabled` only in Live Workout; real runs rely on background `location`+`audio` modes to keep the process alive while locked.
